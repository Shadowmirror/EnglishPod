# Global View - Understanding a Trial

## Key Vocabulary

- **reach a verdict** *phrase* — succeed in making the finding of a jury in a trial
- **guilty** *Adjective* — responsible for doing something bad
- **your honor** *phrase* — a respectful title used to or of certain judges
- **jury** *common noun, singular* — a body of persons sworn to judge
- **accuse of** *phrase* — to charge someone with a crime
- **commit** *principle verb, infinitive* — to do something (usu- ally a crime)
- **prosecution** *common noun, singular* — the lawyers against the defendant
- **pickpocket** *common noun, singular* — one who steals from pockets
- **testimony** *common noun, singular* — a declaration of truth or fact
- **defendant** *common noun, singular* — the person being sued or accused
- **victim** *common noun, plural* — people affected by a painful or sad experi- ence
- **innocent** *Adjective* — not guilty
- **socalled** *Adjective* — refer to something that is not suitable
- **evidence** *common noun, nonvariable* — something that shows the thing is true
- **witness** *common noun, plural* — one who is called on to testify before a court

## Supplementary Vocabulary

- **Acquittal** *common noun, singular* — A jury verdict that a criminal defendant is not guilty, or the find- ing of a judge that the evidence is insufficient to support a conviction.
- **Affidavit** *common noun, singular* — A written or printed statement made under oath.
- **Lawsuit** *common noun, singular* — A legal action started by a plaintiff against a defendant based on a complaint that the de- fendant failed to per- form a legal duty which resulted in harm to the plaintiff.
- **Plea** *verb* — In a criminal case, the defendant’s statement pleading ”guilty” or ”not guilty” in answer to the charges.
- **Warrant** *common noun, singular* — Court authorization, most often for law enforcement officers, to conduct a search or make an arrest.
